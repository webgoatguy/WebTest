How to play
  • Tap one of your own nodes, then tap a nearby point (within 3 king-moves) to grow a new edge there. Tap your selected node again to cancel it — any other tap attempts a move from it.
  • Landing on an empty point claims it. Landing on your own point closes a loop. You can't target a point your opponent already controls, and you can't lay a new edge on top of an existing one (yours or theirs).
  • Your edge's straight path can't pass through any existing node (yours or theirs), and a brand-new node can't land on top of an existing edge. Edges can still cross each other, though — that's how cutting works.
  • If your new edge crosses or touches an opponent's edge, that edge is cut. You can cut up to as many opponent edges in one move as your source node's current degree (its number of existing edges) — a fresh node with no edges yet can't cut anything.
  • Cutting a key edge deletes everything downstream that's no longer connected to that player's home corners — that's the only way to remove an opponent's node.
  • Whoever holds the center point when a turn ends scores a point. First to 30 wins.
