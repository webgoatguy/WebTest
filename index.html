How to play
  • The board is centered on (0,0). Blue starts with two nodes at (-9,0) and (-6,0) joined by an edge; Red starts the same way at (6,0) and (9,0). Tap one of your own nodes, then tap a nearby point (within 3 king-moves) to grow a new edge there. Tap your selected node again to cancel it — any other tap attempts a move from it.
  • Landing on an empty point claims it. Landing on your own point closes a loop. Landing on an opponent's point attacks it: every edge attached to that node gets cut and the node becomes yours.
  • Your edge's path can't pass through one of your own existing nodes, and a brand-new node of yours can't land on one of your own existing edges. You also can't lay a new edge on top of an existing one, yours or theirs. None of this applies to your opponent's stuff — your edge can freely pass through or near it.
  • There's no limit on edge length, but a single move can break at most 1 opponent edge — whether from a plain crossing or from attacking a node with more than one edge attached (that would break too many at once, so it's illegal).
  • An edge placed during your opponent's last turn is invincible (shown with a dashed white glow) — trying to break it is an illegal move. It becomes breakable again once that turn passes.
  • Breaking an edge deletes just that edge — nothing cascades. If that leaves a node with no edges at all, that node is removed too.
  • At the end of every turn, both players' scores are updated: the total area each side currently encloses with their own edges (draw them, flood-fill the outside — whatever never gets touched by the flood fill is "enclosed") is added to that side's running total, not just whoever just moved. A simple branch with no loop encloses nothing; a closed loop encloses whatever it surrounds. The game ends the instant 120 actions have been placed, and whoever has the higher score at that point wins (ties are possible).
  • The "Action" counter at the top counts every single edge placed by either side, in order — Blue's opening edge is action 1, Red's two replies are actions 2 and 3, and so on.
